How four million riders moved into a new app and brand they never asked for, and stayed

Role
Senior Product Designer
Timeline
2019 – 2020
The problem
car2go and DriveNow merged. About four million riders had to move to one app and a new brand, and neither side could feel like the loser.
The work
I led product design for the move: the migration plan, a new vehicle panel, and the new identity, carried from the branding agency into the app.
The result
Riders stayed. The app kept its 4.5★ rating and its weekly riders, and 3.7% more riders confirmed a package.

ContextTwo apps, two fan bases, one merger nobody asked for

In 2019 Daimler and BMW combined their carsharing services into Share Now. The merger was mostly about building one app instead of two, and car2go had the bigger product team behind its app. So the car2go app stayed and DriveNow’s would go. That left the real design problem: bring DriveNow riders into an app they had never opened, and rebrand an app car2go riders liked as it was.

The planMove everyone first, change the name later

There was no big relaunch. First the car2go app quietly became the future app, still in car2go colors: new map pins, a new vehicle panel, packages earlier in the flow, each shipped as an ordinary update. DriveNow riders moved in under their old brand. Then, on one day, everyone switched to Share Now. Alongside, I worked with the branding agency on the new identity and carried it through the app.

The designThe panel every rental hinges on

The vehicle panel is where you decide whether a car is worth the walk. It went through three iterations: the least information first, then legibility, then the balance between panel and map. The result was a vehicle image on top, fuel or battery and walking distance in one line, and one clear button. Versions of that layout later showed up at TIER, Sixt, Miles and Free2Move. Nobody owns a pattern, but it was nice to be early.

TodayStill on the street

On switch day the DriveNow app was retired, and the car2go app changed its name, logo and colors, but not the flow underneath. Share Now is run by Free2Move now, and the map, the vehicle panel and the screens from back then are still there under a new coat of paint. I’d do plenty differently today. It held up anyway.
