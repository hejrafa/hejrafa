Timeline

The problem
Health apps turn tracker data into scores and streaks, want an account in return, and make logging food a chore.
The work
I design and build Akari in SwiftUI. Jan does the research behind every explanation. Two people, from the first idea to the last word.
The result
A free public beta on TestFlight, on its way to the App Store. Health data stays on the iPhone.

ContextAn app we wanted for ourselves

Health matters to me, and so do the trackers that measure it. Talking about them with my friend Jan, now my co-founder, we kept landing on the same thing: a tracker already knows a lot about sleep, heart and movement, but most apps turn that into scores and streaks to keep up with. In August 2026 the idea took shape: a calm way to read that data, and food logging that is actually easy.

The approachPrivacy first, science behind every sentence

Akari needs no account. It reads Apple Health on the iPhone and keeps what it works out right there. The one exception is food: a meal someone types, says or photographs goes through Akari’s server to an AI service, and is not stored. Design and code sit with me, from concept to the App Store setup. Jan, a lawyer, does the research and makes sure everything Akari says has evidence behind it.

The designYour own usual, not a universal target

Each morning Akari reads last night against your own usual nights, and counts yesterday’s food and strain too. Every nutrient gets its own row and symbol, and where there is a limit, Akari marks it.

Logging a meal takes one sentence or a photo. “Oats with banana and a flat white” becomes three foods, each with its portion and what is in it.

A number only matters when it tells you something.

TodayBuilt in SwiftUI, open in beta

With design and code in the same hands, there is no handoff: a spacing value, an animation or a sentence goes from idea to the iPhone in one sitting. Akari is in a free public beta on TestFlight, and what testers say decides what gets built next. Sleep, movement, vitals and goals stay free; food logging, readiness and Health Age become a subscription after 14 days.
